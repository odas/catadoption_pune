Our story

A virtual adoption centre for Pune's indie cats, run by the rescuers who save them.

Virtual Adoption Centre · volunteer-run · not an NGO

Started by a cat feeder with a bag always full of food

My bag is always full of cat food. That is how this started — feeding the community cats near home, getting close enough to a cat to catch it for a neuter or a spay, and coming back the next week to see who was still there.

Then one Diwali the kittens under a car had no mother. Four of them, about a month old, too young to last outside. So they came home with me, and once they were well I started looking for people to adopt them. I posted appeals and got almost nothing back. The established organisations were unreachable. This city has no government shelter. I started @catadoption_pune to home those four and a few others — and then strangers began asking me to post their rescues too.

That is when the real problem showed itself, and it is not a shortage of good homes. It is that there is almost nothing holding up the people doing this work. No reliable information. Nobody answering the phone. Very little written down about keeping a cat in an Indian flat, in this climate, on what is actually sold here. Every rescuer I met had taught themselves alone and assumed that was normal. Most of us simply don't know much about cats yet — I didn't.

Before Pune I fostered for the BC SPCA in Vancouver, failed at it twice and adopted the cats, and later volunteered as a kitten adoption facilitator in Toronto. I have a day job in data. This runs alongside it.

When this site says we, it means the network. The rescuers across Pune and PCMC take cats off the street, pay the vet bills, foster them in their own homes and hand them over themselves — they are the volunteers here. I run the board they post to, answer the DMs, and check that a cat is genuinely ready before it is listed.

The mission is unglamorous and it matters: indie cats, shy cats and older cats make just as wonderful pets as expensive breeds. Most people don't know that yet.

catadoption_pune lists and vets cats placed by trusted rescuers across Pune and PCMC. It is not an NGO. It does not run a shelter or handle urgent rescue cases. What it does is list genuinely good cats and connect them with genuinely good homes.

Adoption here is free. Not as a kindness — a cat is not a commodity, and we would rather not put a number on one. There is something we ask for instead: that a kitten is fed what it actually needs, that the vaccinations happen, and that the cat is neutered or spayed when it is old enough. That is the fee.

Money also changes the conversation. Once someone has paid, the questions a rescuer needs to ask start to feel like an obstacle instead of part of the deal — and those questions are how a cat ends up in the right home rather than merely in a home.

About 120 cats have gone home this way since May 2024. Every one of them was posted with the rescuer's own name and contact, because the person who knows the cat should be the person you talk to.

The network covers Baner, Kothrud, Viman Nagar, Pimple Saudagar, Defence Colony, Kharadi, Pimpri-Chinchwad, Bhavani Peth, Hadapsar, Vishrantwadi — and wherever else good rescuers operate.
