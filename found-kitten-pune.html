You found a kitten outside alone. Leave it be.

Nine times out of ten the mother is a few feet away, waiting for you to go. Here is how to tell, and what to ask yourself before you take one home.

This is the answer we send most often and get asked about again and again, so it is written out properly here. A DM scrolls away.

01She is almost certainly nearby.

A mother cat leaves her litter constantly. She goes to eat, she goes to drink, and when she is moving them she carries one at a time, so for a while there is exactly one kitten sitting where you found it. She also will not come near while a person is standing over them.

"It is more than likely mother cat is nearby."

02They rely on her for everything.

This is the test that actually works. A kitten small enough to need rescuing cannot walk far, cannot keep itself warm and cannot even go to the toilet without its mother. So a kitten trotting about on its own in the rain is not a newborn that has been abandoned. It is a kitten whose mother is nearby.

"Kittens rely on mom for everything. Cannot even pee/poo on their own. Unlikely to be walking around alone in rain."

03So please, leave them be.

Walking away is not doing nothing. It is the thing that gives the kitten its mother back, and it is the most useful move available to most people who message us.

"So please... Leave Them Be."

04Unless it is in danger right now.

A busy road, an open drain, dogs, ants already on it, heavy rain with nowhere dry, or a kitten that is cold and floppy when you pick it up. Then none of the waiting above applies and it needs a vet today. Emergency contacts and NGOs are here.

05A dry corner, and food every day.

A carton on its side under the stairs is a warm dry place. The part that matters is the every day, because a kitten fed on Tuesday and not again until Sunday is worse off than one nobody started feeding.

"Give it a warm, dry place outside & feed it everyday. Let it learn to hunt."

06Dewormed, vaccinated, neutered.

At five months, and it does not have to cost you anything. This is the bit that actually reduces the number of kittens in your lane next summer.

"Get it dewormed, vaccinated & when they are 5 months old get them neutered/spayed through free services by Blue Cross, Project Mau, etc."

07Has the mother died?

This is the question to answer before you pick anything up, and it is a different question from whether the kitten looks alone. If she is dead, or it was dumped by a person and never learnt to hunt, or genuinely nobody is feeding it, then yes, it may need a home.

"So before getting kitten home: Has mom cat passed away?"

08It stops being a kitten in four months.

Food, vaccines, a vet when something goes wrong, and somewhere for it to go when you travel. That is the actual ask, and it is worth answering honestly rather than hopefully.

"If you get a kitten home - be ready for 10-15 years commitment. Can you commit to 10-15 yrs of food, vaccines & other responsibilities of kittens that never learn to hunt?"

09Why it may never get adopted.

None of this is true of indies, and all of it is what people believe. That belief is why a kitten taken home on a good impulse can sit unadopted for a year, and it is why the question above is a real one rather than a lecture.

"Most still think: dirty, has fleas · diseases · not house/litter-trained · not meant for home."

If you have already picked one up

Message us. We are @catadoption_pune on Instagram and DMs are how everything here works. We cannot take the kitten in, and there is no shelter in Pune that takes a healthy one either. What we can usually do is find you the person who knows what they are doing, or a nursing stray who will accept one more.

Four things in the first message save a day of back and forth, and a day matters at this age: a photo of the whole kitten next to your hand for scale; whether its eyes are open and whether it can walk; your area; and whether you can keep it for a few days or genuinely cannot. Both answers to the last one are fine. We just need the true one.
