Can cats live on doodh-roti?

Not as the main meal, and there is one clear reason why. No judgement here — a lot of Pune cats eat this, and a fed cat is better than a hungry one.

By catadoption_pune · September 2026 · Pune & PCMC

A cat needs taurine, and taurine comes from meat. Roti, rice and milk have almost none of it, so a cat living on doodh-roti is slowly running short of something its body cannot make and cannot borrow from anywhere else. That is the whole reason. If doodh-roti is what you can manage right now, keep feeding — the last section is about what you can add when you are able to.

The one thing a cat's body cannot make

Taurine. Cats can't make enough of it themselves, and it comes from meat. A cat short of it for a long time can end up with heart damage and eye damage, and the hard part is that neither shows up early. By the time you can see it, it has been happening for a while.

"Obligate carnivore" is the technical way of saying this. It doesn't mean a cat prefers meat. It means a cat's body is built to run on animal protein and cannot switch to running on anything else, the way ours can.

Dogs manage roti. Cats mostly don't.

Dogs are omnivores, so they digest grains like rice, wheat and oats reasonably well. Cats lack the enzymes to digest those carbs efficiently. So the building's dog doing perfectly fine on roti tells you nothing about the cat, and a cat is not a small dog.

And the milk part

Both adult cats and adult dogs may be lactose intolerant. That usually shows up as a tummy upset or loose motions, which is uncomfortable for the cat and miserable for whoever cleans the floor. Cats do love milk, so this one surprises people.

Kittens are a separate question and a more urgent one, and it's answered on the page about feeding a rescued kitten.

People disagree about cat food, and that's ok

Long-time rescuers disagree with each other about what to feed a cat. The research is thin and it contradicts itself over time (grain-free is one example, and we still don't know). So this page sticks to what is settled and stops there. For the rest, check with a vet you trust.

And us pet-parents notice things too. You see your cat every day, so you'll catch changes a vet may not: the coat is shinier on one diet, or the cat is lethargic on another, or it vomits often. Once in a while is normal (cats bring up hairballs), but more than that is a bad sign.

If doodh-roti is what you have

Small additions count, and you don't have to change everything at once. An egg. A bit of boiled chicken when you're cooking anyway. Anything animal, added to what's already going into the bowl.

Upasana, who has fed and rescued cats in Pune for years and is vegetarian herself, does it like this: curd with chicken powder mixed in, bone broth added to dal, and boiled chicken sometimes. A vegetarian kitchen is not a reason a cat can't be fed properly, and she is the proof of it.

Plenty of people give cats the same food they eat, and honestly, what else are they going to do. Some food and support is better than none, against a life outside with the cars and the monsoon and whatever is going around. That's why we say community cats can have a decent life too. This page isn't asking you to do more than you can. It's just so you know what the gap is, and can close a bit of it when there's a bit to spare.
